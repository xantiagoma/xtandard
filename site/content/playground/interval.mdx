Type two intervals in their [string form](@BASE@/interval/#string-form): `[a,b]`, `(a,b)`, `[a,b)`,
`(a,b]`, unbounded `(-Infinity,5]`, or empty `∅`. Every operation below runs the real
`@xtandard/lib/interval` code in your browser. Filled dots are closed (included) ends; hollow dots
are open ends.

<IntervalPlayground client:visible />

:::note[Things to try]

- **Open vs closed edges**: `[3,10]` minus `(4,6)` gives `[3,4] ∪ [6,10]`. Change B to `[4,6]` and the pieces become `[3,4) ∪ (6,10]`.
- **Discrete vs continuous**: with A `[1,5]` and B `[6,10]`, the union is `null` (there's a gap) for `NumberInterval`, but `[1,10]` for `IntegerInterval`, because no integer lies between 5 and 6. See [discrete vs continuous](@BASE@/interval/semantics/#discrete-vs-continuous-the-epsilon).
- **Gap and span**: for disjoint intervals, `gap` is the hole between them and `span` is the smallest interval that covers both.

:::
